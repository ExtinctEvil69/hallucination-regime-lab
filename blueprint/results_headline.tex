\textbf{Findings at a glance (generated from results).}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=2pt]\item H1, the primary question. Does history help detect the first hallucination? No measurable gain. Filter minus matched static onset AUROC: registry -0.034 [-0.079, +0.015] (locked); -0.008 [-0.035, +0.021] (exploratory); clinical -0.102 [-0.208, -0.002] (locked, 8 test onsets). The answer's own features dominate; filtered risk saturates near 0 or 1.
\item H2. Errors cluster within dossiers under the locked turn/question-type permutation test (registry: 27 H$\to$H transitions vs null 9.8, p = 0.0005, 499 dossiers, 2000 permutations; clinical: 6 vs 1.5, p = 0.0020). A post-hoc within-dossier ordering sensitivity, conditional on each dossier's observed error count, does not reject in either domain (exact enumeration: registry p = 0.159, 35 informative dossiers; clinical p = 0.447, 9 informative). This sensitivity assumes exchangeable turn positions and does not control question-type or turn effects (in a position-only control with no history effect it rejects, p = 2.3e-90). The analyses do not establish observational persistence beyond both dossier difficulty and turn conditions, and they do not establish its absence. Separately, in the eligible erroneous prefixes studied, replacing the previous answer with its verified correction reduced observed next-turn unsupported-answer rates relative to the original-prefix branch (H5).
\item H3 (association). When the requested record is present, the risk of entering H is lower by 0.073 [0.020, 0.129]; referential ambiguity raises it by +0.026 [-0.023, +0.087] (P>0 = 0.82). Covariate model M4 onset AUROC 0.820 vs EM filter 0.772. [M4 NONCONVERGED: max R-hat 1.44, min bulk ESS 143; provisional.]
\item H4 (forecasting the next answer). Integrating over parameters (M3, converged) changes the Brier score relative to the plug-in EM forecast by +0.0040 [+0.0027, +0.0050] on registry and -0.0032 [-0.0038, -0.0024] on clinical (positive = M3 better; exploratory), so the benefit is not consistent across domains; no model beats a constant base-rate forecast (M4 minus base rate -0.0060 [-0.0102, -0.0010]; Brier 0.0503 vs 0.0443). Next-turn hallucinations are rare (about 4\%) and hard to anticipate before the answer exists.
\item H5 (randomised, causal for eligible prefixes). Replacing the earlier wrong answer with the verified one changes next-turn H by -0.120 [-0.182, -0.070] (15 pairs better, 0 worse); replicated on clinical charts: -0.098 [-0.201, -0.028]. A re-grounding reminder is weaker (-0.037 [-0.093, +0.012]) and raises unwarranted UNKNOWN answers by +0.083.
\item Application (clinical guard, 50 held-out charts; hold threshold = Bayes-decision 1/(1+10) = 0.091, which overrides the validation-frozen threshold 1.000). Silent errors (wrong codes not held for review): 2.3\% $\to$ 0.7\% of answers; hold rate 13.0\%, hold precision 0.231, recall 0.818. However, pre-emptive re-grounding (131 turns) did not reduce errors: the guarded H rate was 3.7\% vs 2.3\% (paired difference +0.013 [-0.003, +0.037]). A monitor-only policy (hold, never re-ground) caught 5/7 errors with 31 holds; this is the recommended configuration. Run uses the corrected-sampler posterior (versioned re-run, F11).
\item Red team. Indirect prompt injection raised the H rate from 0.061 to 0.206 (49 answers obeyed the planted instruction); sycophancy pressure raised it on pressured turns to 0.123. Ranking held (M4 onset AUROC 0.81 under injection), but a 5\% false-stop alarm caught no onsets. Detector: corrected sampler, digests recorded in attacks.json provenance (F12); its M4 is NONCONVERGED, so the M4 numbers are provisional.
